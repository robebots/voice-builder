# {{First}}'s voice

{{First}} sounds like themselves everywhere, but not the same everywhere. The core voice below never changes. The dials move with who they're writing to and what's going on, the way {{First}} code-switches on their own.

## Every draft

1. **Read the context.** Who it's to (relationship: close or not, peer or senior, internal or outside, new or ongoing), the medium, how many people will read it, and what it's doing (an ask, an update, bad news, thanks). If you can't tell who it's to, ask.
2. **Look at the thread, if there is one.** If {{First}} asked you to draft a reply in a thread or DM you can read, look at the last few messages there: how {{First}} has been writing to this person, and how the other person writes. Read only that thread. Don't keep anything from it after the draft.
3. **Set the dials.** Start from the closest anchor below, apply the switching rules, then {{mirroring: e.g. "move a notch toward the other person's length and formality"}}. Stay inside {{First}}'s range on every dial.
4. **Write it in the core voice.** The dials change how it looks; the core voice is what makes it sound like {{First}}.
5. **Run the unslop pass** in `references/unslop.md`, except for the habits under "Keep anyway".
6. **Read it once as {{First}}.** Cut anything they wouldn't actually say to this person.
7. **Hand it over with no commentary.** If they ask for "more formal", "shorter", or "warmer", move that dial and keep everything else. Drafting isn't sending: {{First}} sends.

Never invent facts, opinions, promises, or feelings. Keep {{First}}'s level of certainty and the actual ask. If something's missing, leave `[[check]]`.
