# Roles and formal writing

Slack and email show how someone writes day to day. Most of what an agent drafts for them is more formal: PRs, proposals, policies, posts. Use this table to guess which formal writing comes with their role, then confirm it with them.

## How the voice carries into formal writing

- **Carries over:** word choice, directness, where the point goes, how they hedge or don't, how warm they are, what they'd never say.
- **Doesn't carry over:** lowercase, abbreviations, laugh tags, emoji, message bursts, typos. Formal writing uses normal caps, punctuation, and complete sentences.
- **Team rules win.** If their company has a template, style guide, or skill for a document type (a PR template, a proposal skill, a brand guide), the voice only shapes the wording inside it.
- **Unslop always applies** to formal writing, with no "keep anyway" exceptions, except technical terms their field actually uses.

## By role

| Role | Everyday writing | Formal writing an agent will draft | Defaults for the formal register |
|---|---|---|---|
| Engineering | Questions, code review threads, status in channels | PR descriptions, commit messages, tickets, RFCs and design docs, incident writeups, code review comments | What changed, why, how it was checked, what's risky. Exact names and numbers. No selling. |
| Product and design | Feedback, proposals, decisions | Specs and PRDs, design rationale, release notes, research summaries | Problem first, then the decision and what was ruled out. Short sections. |
| Sales | Follow-ups, scheduling, internal deal chatter | Post-call recap emails, proposals, one-pagers, outbound sequences | The buyer's words and problem first, specifics over adjectives, one clear next step. |
| Customer success and support | Replies, escalations, handoffs | Help articles, renewal and QBR notes, escalation emails, incident notices to customers | Plain steps, what happens next and when, no blame, no jargon the customer didn't use. |
| Marketing | Feedback on drafts, campaign coordination | Blog posts, social posts, landing page copy, launch announcements, newsletters | Brand voice guide first. Concrete claims with proof. Their voice shows in the opener and the hook. |
| HR and people | Scheduling, policy questions, sensitive one-on-ones | Policies, company announcements, job descriptions, offer letters, sensitive messages | Clear and kind, no legalese unless required, nothing that reads as a promise unless it is one. Flag anything legal or sensitive for human review. |
| Finance and operations | Approvals, requests for numbers | Budget memos, process docs, vendor emails, board or investor updates | Numbers first, assumptions stated, the decision needed and by when. |
| Leadership | Quick calls, nudges, praise | All-hands updates, strategy memos, org announcements, investor updates | The decision or news in the first line, the why, what changes for the reader. |

If their role isn't here, pick the closest row and say which one you used.
